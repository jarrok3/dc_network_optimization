02 / WHAT MANAGEMENT ASKED

Four claims. One common objective.

Choose how many centres to run and where—and show why the choice survives a full operational evaluation.

Strategy & Planning

“Eight centres cut fixed costs.”

Evaluation of a decision · one cost term

Fewer sites are not automatically cheaper overall. Even DC fixed cost rises from 326.695 M to 334.550 M in the selected 12-to-11-site change.

Delivery Operations

“Longer routes can erase the saving.”

Evaluation of the same decision

Vehicle, fuel, spot and failure costs belong in the same monthly total as leases and staffing—not in a separate argument.

Data Team

“A centre of gravity is optimal.”

Method for choosing · not proof

A fast geographic score can generate candidates. Only routed cost and service can judge the network it recommends.

Field Manager

“Our trucks still need the bridge.”

Claim about model validity

Road detours and access constrain every comparison. Straight-line proximity cannot establish road feasibility.

Decision rule: minimize total monthly cost, conditional on at least 98% before-noon service on every tested DC-day and chain-day.

Evidence: Sections 1, 2 and 5.3 · Units: M = million KRW/month.02 / 12

03 / HOW THE DECISION SUPPORT WORKS

Choose sites once. Plan routes day by day.

The network is chosen before demand is known. Daily routes are chosen after that day’s orders are available.

site-selection + fleet-policy

Upper level

Choose an open-site set and the fleet policy. Search over feasible candidates under a fixed evaluation budget.

q = 2/3 · base fleet policy
→
store-assignment

Connection

Re-solve an exact capacitated assignment LP inside each evaluation. Apply site eligibility and store-count bounds.

Assignment is derived—not 1,970 extra search variables.

→
daily-routing · evaluates the network

Lower level

Route each DC on each sampled demand day. Return vehicle need, kilometres, spot hire and service failures.

These outputs size owned fleets and determine full monthly cost E.

Inputs

Stores and current DCs; leasable-site candidates; cached road distances; demand histories; cost and vehicle limits.

Outputs

Open DCs, store territories, fleet by DC, monthly cost and service, uncertainty intervals, and an auditable evaluation log.

Hierarchical · one-shot/stateless boundary. Site choice changes assignment feasibility; assignment changes the daily routing problem. Routing evaluates the strategic choice. No lease-transition sequence is solved.

Evidence: Sections 1, 2b and I/O schema.03 / 12
